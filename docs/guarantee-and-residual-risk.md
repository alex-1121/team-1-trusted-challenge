# HEALTH-S1: what we guarantee, what we do not, and which attacks we ran

Scenario HEALTH-S1: a hospital gives its emergency-department (ED) visits and lab results to a university research group in a research environment (a known recipient, no public release) (`contracts/HEALTH-S1.json`). Every number below is followed by the file it comes from. Scorecard numbers are our self-assessment from the organisers' `pets-eval`, not the official judging score.

## Why the earlier release was replaced

Our own red team broke the release on main at commit 7cf541b, although the organisers' scorecard gave it composite 0.9709 and linkage re-identification 0.0 (`results/redteam/before-7cf541b.json`, built by main's own code):

- **The dropped date came back.** The release kept only the hour of arrival, but the visits could be put back in arrival order three ways: the file's own row order (dates rebuilt for 0.9975 of rows), sorting by `visit_id`, which was kept as is and is sequential (1.0), and the first `lab_results.lab_id` per visit (1.0 of the 26209 visits with labs). Wherever the hour goes backwards a new day starts, so every date returns.
- **News clips then name patients.** With the rebuilt dates, 10 of the 220 shipped news clips single out one visit, and all 10 are the right patient, so the reader of the clip learns that patient's diagnosis.
- **The patient pseudonym made a fingerprint.** It linked every visit of a patient; age band, sex, municipality and the set of arrival hours were unique for 0.3544 of all patients and 0.8464 of the 9885 patients with more than one visit.
- **The organisers' linkage attacker could not see it.** It reads a column named `date_of_birth` as a date, so an age band such as "30-39" gives it no age, and it reads "HH:00" as today's date, so its time window matches nothing.

## What we guarantee

Each line is a method with its parameters, applied from `contracts/HEALTH-S1.json`, which the selector wrote (`results/selection/HEALTH-S1.json`). The per-column table is `results/health-s1-build/plan.md`.

- **Only what a required task needs is released.** For every column the selector tried every candidate, scored all five required tasks on each and measured how many rows each leaves in groups smaller than 5 over the columns an outsider could know. It kept the most useful candidate under a safety line of 0.05 on that measure, and only if it improves a required task by at least 0.01 over dropping the column; otherwise the column is dropped. Where dropping a column breaks a required task, the gain test is skipped. On ed_visits the line is not met once triage counts as known (0.1848 before the repair, 0.1547 after it); the reasons for visit_id, triage, temp_c, los_minutes and readmit_30d say "line not met" and we do not claim otherwise. The selector's measure and the k repair count different columns: the repair below enforces k of at least 5 on its k-target columns (age band, municipality, arrival hour, plus the lab flag), while the selector's measure also counts triage, so the release ends above the selector's own line (0.1547) and k of at least 5 does not hold once triage is known. Each reason line in the contract carries those numbers. Columns are judged in a fixed order (quasi-identifiers, then sensitive values, then plain values), so the reason of a column judged early shows the table's risk at that point, not the final one: icd10_primary says "under the risk line" at 0.0227, while the table ends at 0.1848 before the repair. Released from ed_visits: visit token, 10-year age band (also as `age`), municipality (8 most common, the rest "other"), arrival hour, triage, primary diagnosis, temperature, length of stay, 30-day readmission flag. From lab_results: visit token, test code, value. Everything else is dropped, including sex, the other vital signs, the secondary diagnosis and disposition; clinical_notes and published_weekly_stats are withheld (`contracts/HEALTH-S1.json`).
- **No direct identifier, no person key.** The national id, name, phone, address, physicians and free text are dropped. No patient pseudonym is released, so visits of one patient cannot be linked; H-U6 reads `readmit_30d` and scores 0.9579 (`results/health-s1-build/scorecard.json`).
- **No order in the release.** `visit_id` in both tables is a keyed token: "T" plus the first 16 hex characters of HMAC-SHA256 under a 32-byte key drawn fresh for every build, never released. The same visit gets the same token in both tables, so the lab join (H-U7 1.0) works, but a token says nothing about order, and tokens of two builds cannot be joined. Both tables are written in a random row order from the operating system's cryptographic source. Red team: dates rebuilt for 0.0002 of rows (`results/health-s1-build/redteam.json`).
- **k of at least 5 and l of at least 2 on every row, also among visits with labs.** k is the number of rows sharing the same quasi-identifier values (a quasi-identifier identifies someone only in combination); l is the number of distinct primary diagnoses in that group. Columns: age band, municipality, arrival hour, plus whether the visit has lab results, because the release shows it. This holds on these k-target columns only; once triage is known as well, it does not (see the bullet above). Scorer: k<5 share 0.0, min k 5, median k 55, 1691 groups over 46398 rows (`results/health-s1-build/scorecard.json`). l-diversity: no group with fewer than 2 distinct diagnoses (`results/health-s1-build/checks/l_diversity.json`). Joined with lab results, over the quasi-identifiers, no visit sits in a group of fewer than 5 visits (`results/health-s1-build/checks/joined_k.json`).
- **Only the rows at risk were coarsened, and they say so.** 3746 rows (0.0807) missed the target after protection. They got a 20-year age band; 2584 still at risk got a 3-hour arrival block, written as a range such as "06:00 to 08:59" so that no one reads it as an exact hour; 737 got municipality "other"; the last 59 had the three columns suppressed; no row was dropped (`results/health-s1-build/repair_report.json`). The step order was chosen by scoring all 6 orders (`results/health-s1-build/repair_search.md`).
- **The organisers' own attacker, made band-aware, finds nothing.** Rerun with the age read from the band and the hour-only time left out (it cannot be placed on a date): re-identification 0.0 for the news clips, social posts and public directory (`results/health-s1-build/redteam.json`).
- **No plain hash and no identifier pattern.** No released value equals the MD5, SHA-1 or SHA-256 of an original value (`results/health-s1-build/redteam.json`). The organisers' regex scan found no residual direct identifiers in the 7 files of the output (`results/health-s1-build/check.txt`).

## What the gate enforces

The lines above are measured, and since the gate change they also block the build. `build` starts only under a policy the data steward signed: it verifies `policy/HEALTH-S1.policy.json` against `policy/allowed_signers` with `ssh-keygen -Y verify`, and refuses on a missing signature, changed bytes, a wrong signer or no `ssh-keygen`. It also refuses a contract whose sha256 is not the one the policy allows for the selected attacker assumption, so the build runs the contract the steward signed for and no other. `evaluate` in `src/pets_transformer/redteam.py` reads every limit from that policy, decides before any file is copied, and refuses when:

- **An attack fails or could not run.** Thresholds as in `policy/HEALTH-S1.policy.json` (order attack 0.05, news linkage 0.0, person fingerprint 0.05, band-aware linkage 0.01, plain hash 0.0). An attack that had inputs to work on but no result is a failure, for example a release with an hour column and a missing adversary file. Not applicable needs a stated reason: on HEALTH-S1 the fingerprint attack is not applicable because the only repeating key is the visit token in `lab_results`, a table with no quasi-identifier.
- **k or l misses the contract's target.** The scorer's min k on `ed_visits` under 5, the joined min k on the k columns under 5, or fewer than 2 distinct diagnoses in a group.
- **A check did not run.** With a k target, the informed attacker must run, l-diversity when the contract names a sensitive column or l above 1, joined k when it names join flags. Only files this run produced count.
- **A required task is missing or under 0.5,** with the required list taken from the organisers' scenario definition.
- **An identifier is found.** Any finding of the regex scan, a scan that did not run, or a direct-identifier or free-text leakage risk above zero in the scorecard.
- **The folder holds something else.** Any CSV the contract does not name (`plan.csv` stays in the protected folder and is never copied). A table the contract withdraws loses its old CSV and row map on rebuild.

After a passing gate the build writes `release_record.json` to the submission folder and to `results/`: the policy's sha256, the signature file name, the contract's sha256, the sha256 of every released file, the recipient, the purpose, the agreement reference, the recipient's duties, what is enforced after release, and the residual risks from the checks.

The gate does not enforce the informed attacker's result or the exact-code join k below: both are reported, neither blocks the build.

## What we do not guarantee

- **An attacker who already knows the diagnosis (unless the team switches it off).** One who knows a visit's diagnosis group, age band, municipality and arrival hour at the release's precision singles out exactly one row for 0.0971 of the 46398 visits, 0.9294 of those correctly, counting every coarsened row as someone else (the attacker's best case); against the original at the same precision it is 0.1282 (`results/health-s1-build/checks/informed_attacker.json`). The attacker then sees that visit's length of stay, temperature and lab values. The contract `contracts/HEALTH-S1-informed.json` closes it: the repair also coarsens every row that is unique once the diagnosis group is known, and the informed attacker singles out 0.0; see the open decision below for its cost.
- **Exact diagnosis codes on the lab join.** Counting the exact diagnosis code in the key, 0.7062 of visits with lab results sit in groups of fewer than 5 such visits (`results/health-s1-build/checks/joined_k.json`). Diagnosis is the sensitive value, not a quasi-identifier, so k usually leaves it out; we report it for an attacker who knows the code.
- **Three gaps in the selector's rule.** A plain category with more than 20 distinct values is never measured; a sensitive column the detector labels plain survives if it gains at least 0.01 on any task, and if it is a small category it counts as a key rather than as something to protect; a column whose usefulness is spread over several tasks each under 0.01 is dropped (`plans/2026-11-07-selector-v2-measured-risk.md`, limitations (c), (f), (g)).
- **Anything after the release.** The policy lists the recipient's duties: no re-identification, and no linkage with the public directory, the news clips or the social posts of this scenario's threat model. `enforced_after_release` is "none": no code enforces those duties once the files leave, so they hold only as far as the agreement with the recipient does. The `agreement_ref` of every policy says no real agreement exists, because this is a synthetic scenario.
- **That the signer is the steward, beyond the key.** A valid signature proves that a key listed in `policy/allowed_signers` signed the policy. That file sits in the repo, so anyone who can change it can change who counts as the steward. Reviewing changes to it is the only control today. A policy also has no expiry: an older signed version keeps verifying if someone restores it.
- **Clinical values are released as they are.** Diagnosis, triage, temperature, length of stay and lab values are exact because the study needs them.
- **Attackers we did not build.** We ran the shipped attackers, a band-aware rerun of them, and our own order, news-clip, fingerprint and hash attacks. A stronger attacker with other outside data was not tested.
- **Tasks pay a price.** H-U8 (arrival curve) falls to 0.9446: the scorer cannot place the 2584 rows written as hour ranges, so they leave its curve; H-U4 to 0.9549; H-U6 to 0.9578 (`results/health-s1-build/scorecard.json`). Without the lab join flag the composite would be 0.992 instead of 0.9866, with 0.055 of visits with labs in groups of fewer than 5 (`results/health-s1-noflag-build/checks/joined_k.json`). Closing the informed attacker costs composite 0.9647 (`results/health-s1-informed-build/`).
- **No dates, so no seasonality.** A researcher cannot study weekly or seasonal patterns on this release.
- **Not the organisers' suggested patient key.** Their suggested targets ask for a consistent pseudonymous patient key (`metadata/scenarios.json`). With one, 0.2935 of patients are unique by fingerprint and our gate refuses the release (`results/health-s1-pseudonym-build/redteam.json`).

So the release depends on the research environment and the agreement with a known recipient, as HEALTH-S1 assumes. It is not fit for public release.
